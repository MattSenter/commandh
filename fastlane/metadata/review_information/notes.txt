No sign-in, account, or network connection is required to use CommandH.

The system will not hide an app when it is the only regular app left showing:
pressing Command-H on it does nothing. CommandH makes Command-H work in that
case too. It is a menu-bar app; the settings window opens on first launch and
from the menu-bar icon.

To try it:
1. Launch CommandH and leave Enabled on.
2. Hide every app but one (for example Option-Command-H in Finder, then
   Command-H in the remaining apps), leaving one app such as Safari showing.
3. Press Command-H in that last app. Without CommandH nothing happens; with
   CommandH running it hides and the desktop is visible.

How: while at most one other app is showing, CommandH switches its activation
policy to regular and orders front a 1x1, nearly transparent, click-through
window, so the last app is no longer the last regular app and its own
Command-H hide succeeds. CommandH's Dock icon is visible only during that time
and disappears once two or more apps are showing again. Bring hidden apps back
from the Dock or with Command-Tab as usual.

Closing the settings window or pressing Command-Q there keeps CommandH running
in the menu bar, so a stray keystroke never disables it. "Quit CommandH" in the
menu-bar menu or the settings window quits it.

The app runs in the App Sandbox with no other entitlements. It uses only public
APIs (NSWorkspace running-application state and notifications, NSApplication
activation policy, SMAppService for the optional Open at Login). It requests no
Accessibility, Screen Recording, or Input Monitoring permission, does not read
keystrokes or screen contents, makes no network connections, and collects no
data. There is no in-app purchase.
